# Customer testimonial template - 5-beat honest-attribution formula

Owner: {{ YOUR_NAME }}. Status: TEMPLATE - reference for every customer testimonial across every surface.

This template enforces the 5-beat honest-attribution structure defined in `01-canon/cocktail-recipes.md` "The honest-attribution testimonial cocktail". The frame protects Wall 1 by separating OUTCOME (owned by customer discipline) from MECHANISM (owned by the product).

Use this template when:

- Drafting a hypothetical testimonial for a PDP placeholder
- Editing a real customer review before it ships to the PDP / IG / TikTok
- Briefing a creator to script a testimonial
- Soliciting a review via day-7 post-purchase email (see `03-touchpoint-copy/emails/review-request.md`)
- Pulling a quote for the founder section of the website

## The 5 beats

### Beat 1 - Discipline + goal (customer-owned)

The customer states what they were doing and why. Both the discipline and the goal belong to them.

> "I have been {{ DISCIPLINE_VERB }} for {{ TIMEFRAME }} to {{ GOAL }}."

Examples of `{{ DISCIPLINE_VERB }}` (concrete verbs, not abstract identities): cutting, bulking, running, fasting, hitting macros, hitting protein, skipping {{ CATEGORY }}, training for {{ EVENT }}, recovering from {{ INJURY_OR_EVENT }}.

Examples of `{{ GOAL }}`: a specific number, a specific event, a specific deadline, a specific feeling. NOT a generic "be healthier" / "feel better".

### Beat 2 - Honest disclaimer (what the product did NOT cause)

Explicit refusal to credit the product for the outcome.

> "The {{ MEASURABLE_RESULT }} came from {{ DISCIPLINE }}, not from {{ BRAND_NAME }}. {{ ATTRIBUTION_DETAIL }}."

`{{ ATTRIBUTION_DETAIL }}` examples: "My training did. My consistency did. My coach did." / "I just kept showing up." / "I had to count every gram and refuse every drink."

This beat is non-negotiable. Without it, the testimonial becomes a Wall-1 product claim. With it, the testimonial earns the right to compliment the product in Beat 3.

### Beat 3 - Specific failure mode the product PREVENTED (adherence-rescue)

The customer names the exact moment they would have quit without the product. This is where the product gets its credit.

> "What I would have failed on was {{ SPECIFIC_FAILURE_MODE }} - {{ CONCRETE_DETAIL_OF_THAT_MOMENT }}. That is what {{ BRAND_NAME }} caught."

Examples of `{{ SPECIFIC_FAILURE_MODE }}`: "boredom by week 3", "the third {{ MEAL }} of the week tasting like nothing", "the moment I would have ordered {{ FAILURE_BEHAVIOUR }}", "the airport day when I was about to abandon the routine".

This is the load-bearing beat. The whole testimonial earns its place by being SPECIFIC here. Vague "it helped" / "highly recommend" fails the structure.

### Beat 4 (optional) - Treat-within-the-frame

A permitted indulgence the product makes liveable inside the discipline.

> "Now {{ TREAT }} is part of the routine instead of a cheat."

This beat is optional but powerful. It signals the brand is not ascetic - it is liveable. Use it when the testimonial allows.

### Beat 5 (optional) - Community identity close

The customer self-identifies with the brand community.

> "I am {{ COMMUNITY_NAME_SINGULAR }} now."

Use only when the brand has a named community (see `00-foundations/positioning.md` POS-LICENSES list). Skip if the community name is not yet locked.

## Worked example

> Beat 1: "I have been cutting for 14 months to get from 85 kg to my target weight before a deadline I set myself."
>
> Beat 2: "The 18 kg came off because I count macros every day and I do not skip the gym. {{ BRAND_NAME }} did not cause that."
>
> Beat 3: "What would have stopped me was the same boiled-chicken-on-rice every day. I have tried cutting before. By week 3 the food tastes like nothing and I order takeaway and the whole thing falls apart. {{ BRAND_NAME }} is the thing that made the necessary meal taste like a meal."
>
> Beat 4: "I keep berries on it now. That used to be a cheat. Now it is dinner."
>
> Beat 5: "I am {{ COMMUNITY_NAME_SINGULAR }} now."

## Reject patterns (do not publish these)

| Pattern | Why it fails | Safe rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| "{{ BRAND_NAME }} helped me lose 18 kg." | Product as cause of outcome. Wall-1 violation. | "I lost 18 kg through {{ DISCIPLINE }}. {{ BRAND_NAME }} kept the meal from getting boring." |
| "I had no energy until I tried {{ BRAND_NAME }}." | Medicinal claim. Wall-1 violation. | "My meals tasted better, so I stopped skipping them. {{ BRAND_NAME }} made them taste like food again." |
| "{{ BRAND_NAME }} works." | Vague-benefit. No structure. Useless. | Use the full 5-beat formula. Send back for rewrite. |
| "Highly recommend." | No specificity. Provides no social proof. | Reject. Solicit a real review. |
| "{{ BRAND_NAME }} is the best {{ CATEGORY }} I have tried." | Category comparison. Wall-2 violation. | "{{ BRAND_NAME }} is what finally clicked for me." |
| "5 stars!" with no body | The widget will display this but it carries no narrative. | Solicit the 5-beat narrative explicitly in the day-7 email. |

## How to use this template at scale

1. **Day-7 post-purchase email** (`03-touchpoint-copy/emails/review-request.md`) prompts the customer with the 5 beats explicitly. Most customers will fill 3 of 5 beats; that is fine.
2. **Manual edit** before publishing on PDP / IG / TikTok. Match the customer's voice; do not put words in their mouth. If a review fails the Wall-1 check, ask the customer to edit (do not edit unilaterally on their behalf).
3. **Sort the 5-beat reviews to the top** by default on PDP. Reviews that fill all 5 beats are the most useful social proof.
4. **Briefing creators**: open the creator brief with this template (see `08-templates/creator-brief-template.md`). Provide 3 worked examples so they internalize the structure.
