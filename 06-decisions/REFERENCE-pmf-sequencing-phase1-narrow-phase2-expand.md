# REFERENCE - PMF sequencing pattern (Phase 1 narrow, Phase 2 expand)

Owner: this is a reference / pattern document, not a brand-specific decision. Status: **canonical pattern**, do not delete.

This file documents the rule that scopes which ICP slices a brand addresses in Phase 1 customer-facing copy versus Phase 2 expansion copy. The pattern is captured here so future Claude sessions and contributors apply it consistently when a brand has done category-anchor work and needs to protect it during launch.

## The rule

**Phase 1 customer-facing copy addresses your narrowest core ICP only. Phase 2 expansion copy widens to friend-of-friend / adjacent-audience adoption channels after the Phase 1 base validates the category-anchor reframe.**

The non-core-ICP friend-of-friend social-discovery channel is real and observable in pre-launch sample distribution. It just stays out of Phase 1 hero / PDP / paid social / IG / email / press copy. Surfacing it in Phase 1 collapses the category-anchor reframe.

## When to use this pattern

Apply this pattern when ALL of the following are true:

1. Your brand has done category-anchor work (see `REFERENCE-category-anchor-reframe.md`) - the customer mental category has been intentionally moved away from the legal / inherited category.
2. Your pricing depends on the new mental category holding (the reference class anchors the price tolerance).
3. You observe pre-launch evidence of organic adoption among non-core-ICP audiences (e.g., samples shared with friends who liked the product).
4. Surfacing those non-core-ICP voices in Phase 1 copy would re-anchor the category back to the old default (because those audiences have no protocol-stack / discipline-budget / commitment-budget to slot the product into).

Without all four, sequencing may not be load-bearing. With all four, sequencing protects the category-anchor work that drives the price tolerance.

## Why the sequencing is load-bearing

Phase 1 acquisition copy sets the brand's category anchor in every new visitor's head. If the testimonial wall, hero copy, PDP, and paid social all show ONLY core-ICP voices, the category-anchor reframe holds. If they show a mix of core-ICP and non-core-ICP voices, the mental category collapses to the old default because non-core-ICP voices have no reference class to slot the new category into.

This is not about excluding non-core-ICP customers (anyone who finds the brand and chooses to buy is welcomed). It is about which voices surface in Phase 1 customer copy.

Phase 2 introduces the friend-of-friend story AFTER the Phase 1 base validates the category-anchor in the wild. By then, the wall already leads with core-ICP voices; non-core-ICP voices added later sit ON TOP of the validated base rather than alongside it as undifferentiated peers.

## How to do it

1. **Define your core ICP** in `00-foundations/positioning.md`. The narrowest slice of your audience where the pain-fit reality is unambiguous and the category-anchor lands hardest.

2. **Define your Phase 2 expansion path**. Which adjacent audiences are observable as adopting organically? Friend-of-friend (the core-ICP recommends to non-core-ICP), category-curiosity (general-foodie tries the discipline product), context-shift (the customer's life stage changes to bring them into the ICP)?

3. **Set a Phase 1 -> Phase 2 transition trigger**. This should be data-driven, not calendar-driven. Examples:
   - Launch + 90 days OR N active subscribers (whichever later)
   - Net Promoter Score from core-ICP customers hits target
   - Brand SEO entity authority on the new category established
   - First press wave with category-anchor framing landed

4. **Scope your Phase 1 copy surfaces**. Hero / PDP h1 / paid social / IG / TikTok / email subject lines / press pitches / testimonial wall - all address core-ICP only. Non-core-ICP voices do not appear here in Phase 1.

5. **Document non-core-ICP organic adoption in evidence**. Even though those voices do not surface in Phase 1 copy, capture them - they become Phase 2 testimonial inventory. Pre-launch sample-distribution data + first 30 days of organic UGC are gold for Phase 2.

6. **Plan the Phase 2 expansion wave**. When the transition trigger fires, ADD non-core-ICP testimonials ON TOP of the core-ICP base. Do not replace. The wall continues to lead with core-ICP voices; non-core-ICP voices are additional cards.

## What this rule does NOT do

- Does not exclude non-core-ICP customers from buying. The exclusion is on which voices surface in Phase 1 customer copy, not on who is welcomed as a customer.
- Does not work as a permanent narrow-only strategy. The rule is a Phase 1 protective strategy; Phase 2 widening is part of the rule, not an alternative to it.
- Does not work for brands without category-anchor work upstream. If your customer mental category and legal category are the same, sequencing has no load-bearing function.
- Does not work as a copy-surface-by-surface mixed strategy. The sequencing applies consistently across all Phase 1 surfaces - PDP narrow, paid social narrow, IG narrow, press narrow. Mixed-by-surface sequencing confuses the customer who sees the brand at multiple touchpoints.

## How to verify the sequencing held

Three checks at 30 / 60 / 90 days post-launch:

1. **Customer mental category drift**. Pull 20 recent customer reviews / UGC and tag them as "category-anchored to new category" or "category-anchored to old default". If more than 30% drift back to old default within 60 days, the sequencing may not be holding (or other surfaces are leaking).

2. **Net Promoter Score by ICP slice**. Core-ICP NPS should outperform non-core-ICP NPS in Phase 1. If they are similar, the brand may already be Phase 2-ready earlier than the trigger calendar suggests.

3. **Phase 2 readiness signal**. At transition trigger, audit the testimonial inventory + UGC + first press wave for category-anchor adherence. If the category-anchor is unambiguous in the public mind, expand to Phase 2. If still fragile, hold sequencing for another 30-60 days.

## How to extend the rule

If your brand later spans multiple Phase 1 ICP slices (e.g., a product line targeting two distinct core-ICP segments simultaneously), apply the sequencing rule per-segment. Each Phase 1 segment has its own narrowness rule + Phase 2 expansion path.

If your brand later changes its category-anchor (e.g., a strategic pivot that shifts the customer mental category to a different reference class), the sequencing rule changes with it. Re-run the audit; do not assume the prior sequencing survives a category-anchor change.

If your brand observes Phase 2 audiences adopting BEFORE the transition trigger fires, that is a signal worth analysing. Either the category-anchor is more robust than expected (good - consider earlier Phase 2 expansion) or the Phase 1 acquisition channels are leaking into Phase 2 audiences (also informative - audit which channels).

- Crossing the Chasm (Geoffrey Moore, 1991) - the early-adopter / early-majority gap requires distinct go-to-market motions per segment
- Sutherland _Alchemy_ ch. 4 - satisficing-at-acquisition vs maximising-at-acquisition; narrow Phase 1 framing reduces decision friction for the core ICP
- Recurly subscription retention data (2024) - first 60 days decide everything for subscription brands; protecting the category-anchor in those 60 days is high-leverage
