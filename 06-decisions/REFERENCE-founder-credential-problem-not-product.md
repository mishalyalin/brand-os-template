# REFERENCE - founder credential is problem-anchored, never product-anchored

Owner: this is a reference / pattern document, not a brand-specific decision. Status: **canonical pattern**, do not delete.

This file documents the rule that governs the founder-credential paragraph wherever it appears in customer-facing copy. The pattern is captured here so future Claude sessions and contributors apply it consistently across hero, manifesto, About page, founder essay, press boilerplate, and welcome-flow founder-pivot email.

## The rule

**Founder credentials describe what the founders DID and what PROBLEM they solved. They never describe what the PRODUCT IS.**

The product reveal comes later in the same surface, after the problem frame has been established in the reader's head. The founder credential's job is to earn the reader's trust that the founders are competent on the problem, not to tell the reader what the product is.

## Why this matters

Most early-stage brands write the founder credential like this:

> _"We researched ancient X traditions and perfected a Y for Z customers."_

This construction front-loads the product (X, Y) inside what should be a problem statement. Three failure modes follow:

1. **Category-anchor re-entry**. If you have done the work to reframe the customer-side category (see `REFERENCE-category-anchor-reframe.md`), and then your founder credential mentions the old category noun, you have undone the reframe inside the very paragraph that is supposed to anchor the brand's authority. The reader files your product back in the old category.
2. **Problem invisibility**. The reader does not know yet what problem the brand solves, so a product description has nowhere to land. The credential reads as "we made a thing" without the reader caring why.
3. **Lower trust**. Credentials that lead with the product feel like sales copy. Credentials that lead with the problem feel like founders who understand the customer.

## The pattern (verb-led, problem-named)

Apply the Sutherland _Alchemy_ verb-not-noun lesson to the founder credential paragraph:

- The founder credential answers: **WHO** (the founders), **WHAT THEY DID** (verb-led action), **FOR WHOM** (the customer segment they solved the problem for).
- The founder credential does NOT answer: what the product is, where it came from, how it is made, what is inside it.

Those product-anchored answers come AFTER the problem-frame is locked in the reader's head, usually in a later paragraph or a separate block.

## Test on every founder-credential draft

Read the paragraph. Ask:

1. Does this paragraph describe the founders' work in terms of THE PROBLEM, or in terms of THE PRODUCT?
2. If a stranger read this paragraph and stopped there, would they know what problem we solve, or would they only know what we made?
3. If you removed every product noun from this paragraph, would it still carry the founder authority? (It should.)

If any answer points to product-anchored framing, rewrite to problem-anchored.

## Before-after examples

| Anti-pattern (product-anchored) | Safe (problem-anchored) |
|---|---|
| "We researched ancient salt and spice traditions" | "We researched methods that work for the customer segment we are solving for" |
| "We perfected an Ayurvedic recipe" | "We refined a method that survives the actual conditions our customer lives in" |
| "We blended Himalayan salt with rare herbs" | "We packaged what worked into something portable" |
| "We sourced the world's best seasonings" | "We tested approaches until we found one that survives modern training and modern travel" |
| "We perfected a 5000-year-old salt recipe" | "We found the method disciplined eaters used for centuries and tested it against modern life" |
| "We developed a tea blend with rare adaptogens" | "We developed a method for people who cannot afford the afternoon crash" |
| "We engineered a fitness mat with antimicrobial coating" | "We engineered a surface that does not become a problem at month 3 of daily use" |
| "We designed a leather wallet with RFID blocking" | "We designed a wallet for the year-long carry, not the seasonal Instagram moment" |

The right-side construction in every row leads with a verb, names the problem the founders solved, and delays the product reveal. The product is revealed (as needed) in a separate paragraph or a downstream block on the same surface.

## What this rule does NOT do

- It does not ban product nouns from the surface entirely. Product nouns are fine in the technical block, the ingredient panel, the spec page, the description below the fold. They are banned ONLY in the founder credential paragraph.
- It does not require the credential to be vague. "We refined a method that works for disciplined eaters" is specific (a method, for disciplined eaters) without naming the product. Specificity attaches to the problem, not the product.
- It does not work if the brand has no actual problem story. If the founders' work was "we made a nicer version of a thing the market already has," there is no problem frame to lead with, and the brand has a positioning problem upstream of this rule.

## How to extend the rule

If your brand later adds new founder voices (co-founders coming on camera, advisory voices joining the brand), apply this rule to each new voice independently. Every founder credential, regardless of who is speaking, leads with the problem, not the product.

If your brand later changes the problem it solves (a strategic pivot), the founder credential changes with it. Old credentials become evidence in `05-evidence/` (for historical reference) rather than active customer copy.
